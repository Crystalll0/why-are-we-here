
# Process


## Tools Used

I used ChatGPT to clarify the assignment requirments, basic programming concepts, and the GitHub workflow, discuss task details, and check whether my ideas and understanding were correct. It also helped me brainstorming, explore possible directions, organise the structure, and find relevant research. Through repeated conversations, I gradually clarified my position, added my own ideas and experiences, and wrote a draft. I then asked for feedback and continued discussing, revising, and refining my ideas. After completing the essay, I used AI to help check the citation formatting and Grammarly to check grammar and wording.

## What I Kept and Why

I kept the phrase “understand enough to participate” because it clearly expressed my goal: learning enough programming to discuss and modify AI-generated code without needing to become an expert.

I also kept the two suggested research papers. The distinction between using AI for “acceleration” and “exploration” helped me explain the value of exchanging ideas with it. The research on AI’s uneven capabilities added a critical perspective: even when a tool is useful, its capabilities still need to be tested and its outputs evaluated.

## What I Rejected and Why

I removed the AI-suggested interactive extension of my previous capstone project. Although it connected programming with my design background, I found the imagined interaction difficult to explain with my current programming knowledge. It also took up too much space, and I could not clearly connect it to my main reason for learning programming.

Instead, I focused on my actual experience: because I understand design, I can discuss and revise visual work with AI, but I struggle to do the same with code. I added my earlier experience of learning programming and using simple expressions in After Effects. These changes made the argument more personal and easier for me to explain clearly.
